Starting Forall Labs

14 October 2026 · Sajjad P. Savoji

Forall Labs is an open research community in AI. It started because the gap between who can do research and who is allowed to kept getting harder to ignore.

The problem this is a response to

If you want to do serious AI research, the usual route is an institution: a university group, a company lab, somewhere with compute and a name. That route is narrow, and getting narrower. Plenty of people who could contribute are outside it — between degrees, in industry jobs that are not research, in countries without a well-funded department nearby, or simply without the affiliation that makes a submission get read.

Meanwhile a growing share of the field's most consequential work happens inside organisations that are not obliged to show anyone how it was done. That is a bad arrangement for a technology this general.

What we are trying to be

An open research community with four commitments: open by default, chosen by who it helps, independent and democratic, and credit for everyone. Those are on the front page because they are meant to be held against us.

Concretely: anyone can join, anyone can propose a project, projects are staffed by whoever shows up, and everything gets published — including the work that did not pan out.

What the first year should produce

Not very much, measured in papers. The honest goal for year one is a working community and two or three projects that actually finish, with code and data that someone else can run. A lab that publishes three reproducible things is more useful than one that announces thirty.

We would rather be judged on that than on a launch announcement.

Where it stands today

The lab is new. There are no papers yet, no running projects, and a community of approximately one. The first round of proposals is open, and the research themes are set but not frozen — members will change them.

If any of this sounds like something you want to exist, the useful thing you can do is join or propose a project.
